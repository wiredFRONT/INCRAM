# Open baselines exist; nobody joins them

State of the world: 12 November 2026. Labels used throughout: **[P]** = read from the owner's own page, repo or spec on that date; **[U]** = the researchers marked it UNVERIFIED (reason given); **[S]** = secondary source only (law firm, tracker, mirror, vendor claim); **[I]** = researcher inference, not a sourced fact.

The raw material for stigops-AI is importable and the combination is unclaimed, but three planning assumptions need correcting. NIST content is the cleanest import (SP 800-53 Rev 5.2.0 ships as OSCAL under a US public-domain statement plus CC0), OpenSSF, MITRE and the language/framework docs are permissive, and most of OWASP is CC BY-SA 4.0, which forces per-record licensing and physical separation of share-alike content from everything else; CIS, PCI DSS, ISO, SOC 2 and the Secure Controls Framework are cite-only. The public-domain basis for DISA STIG/SRG text was **not** confirmed from a DISA page, because cyber.mil is now a JavaScript-only Salesforce site, and no DISA policy on third-party modification was found. A lean custom schema field-mapped to XCCDF 1.2 is sound if it copies XCCDF's merge semantics and not just its field names, treats DISA rule IDs as revision-stamped aliases, and accepts that four header fields have no XCCDF home. Raw STIG lookup for AI agents is already taken, twice for free, so the defensible ground is stack applicability, the four-layer join and plain-language delivery to non-developers; no project found combines those. No upstream offers a push channel, so one person keeps the library current only with a polling registry, content hashes and loud failure on silent breakage. The regulatory survey at the end is factual, not legal advice, and **most of its entries come from law-firm and tracker summaries, not official texts**.

## NIST imports cleanly; OWASP's share-alike sets the packaging rule

### Importable under public-domain or permissive terms

NIST is the lowest-risk source. Its Technical Series terms disclaim US copyright for employee-authored works and grant a worldwide, royalty-free right to reprint in all formats, extending to derivative works, with a requested credit line and a caveat that some NIST-published works have third-party authors **[P]** ([NIST licensing statements](https://www.nist.gov/open/copyright-fair-use-and-licensing-statements-srd-data-software-and-technical-series-publications)). The OSCAL content repository goes further with an explicit CC0 1.0 waiver **[P]** ([oscal-content LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/usnistgov/oscal-content/main/LICENSE.md)). **SP 800-53 Rev 5.2.0 (27 August 2025)** is published in OSCAL JSON, XML and YAML **[P]** ([CSRC](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/final)). SSDF v1.1 remains the final version; v1.2 (SP 800-218 Rev. 1) is an Initial Public Draft from 17 December 2025 and CSRC lists no final **[P]**, so whether it went final by November 2026 is **[U]** ([CSRC SSDF](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/ssdf), [IPD](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/218/r1/ipd)). SP 800-218A is final (26 July 2024), PDF only **[P]** ([CSRC](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/218/a/final)). AI RMF 1.0 is under revision with no new version or date announced, and AI 600-1 dates from 26 July 2024 **[P]** ([NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework)).

Two researchers reported different format pictures for NIST and both are preserved. The licensing researcher recorded SSDF as PDF plus XLSX and AI RMF as PDF with machine-readable Playbook formats **[U]**. The monitoring researcher found that the oscal-content README lists an OSCAL catalog for AI RMF 1.0 and that an undocumented CPRT export endpoint returns JSON for SP 800-218 v1.1 (767 KB) and SP 800-53 5.2.0 (14.1 MB) **[P, endpoint undocumented]** ([oscal-content README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/usnistgov/oscal-content/main/README.md), [CPRT export](https://csrc.nist.gov/extensions/nudp/services/json/nudp/framework/version/sp_800_53_5_2_0/export/json?element=all)). The practical reading: SSDF and AI RMF are more machine-readable than the PDFs suggest, but the CPRT route has no stability guarantee.

DISA content is structurally the best fit (native rule-per-record XCCDF with check, fix and CCI) and legally the least confirmed. The only DISA-authored statement retrieved is the library compilation readme (V2R1, 6 August 2024), which marks the "U_" compilation for public release and says compilations may be used and distributed like individually downloaded documents; it is silent on modification **[P]** ([DISA readme](https://dl.dod.cyber.mil/wp-content/uploads/stigs/pdf/U_STIG_Library-zip_Readme_V2R1.pdf)). A DISA statement that the content is public domain or modifiable is **[U]**; the 17 U.S.C. 105 argument is a general-law inference **[I]**. All DISA version pins (ASD STIG V6R5, 30 September 2026, and the SRG family) come from a third-party mirror **[S]** ([cyber.trackr.live](https://cyber.trackr.live/stig/index)). CISA publishes a marks policy but no copyright statement for its publications, and joint guidance with foreign partners carries no reuse statement, so CISA redistribution is **[U]** ([CISA IP policy](https://cisa.gov/intellectual-property-policy)).

### Share-alike sources

OWASP ASVS 5.0.0 (May 2025), the GenAI project's LLM and Agentic Top 10s, and the Cheat Sheet Series are all CC BY-SA 4.0 **[P]** ([ASVS README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OWASP/ASVS/master/README.md), [GenAI LLM Top 10](https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/), [CheatSheetSeries](https://github.com/OWASP/CheatSheetSeries)). Any record derived from them must itself be offered under CC BY-SA 4.0, so the library needs a per-record `license` field and per-source packaging that keeps share-alike content separable from CC0/Apache content and from any paid tier **[I]**. The Python Packaging User Guide is CC BY-SA 3.0 **[P]** ([PyPA](https://github.com/pypa/packaging.python.org)).

Three source conflicts stand unresolved. The OWASP Top 10:2025 site footer says CC BY 3.0 Unported while the repo LICENSE file is CC BY-SA 4.0 **[P, both]**; complying with BY-SA 4.0 is the conservative reading ([site](https://top10.owasp.org/2025/), [LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OWASP/Top10/master/LICENSE)). On the LLM Top 10 edition, the licensing researcher found genai.owasp.org presenting **2025** as current, while the monitoring researcher read the legacy repo README stating that development moved to `GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10` and that a **2026 edition was published 4 August 2026** **[P, both; new repo's tags not read]** ([legacy README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OWASP/www-project-top-10-for-large-language-model-applications/main/README.md)). ASVS JSON was listed as unconfirmed by the formats researcher and confirmed live in-repo (149 KB) by the monitoring researcher ([ASVS JSON](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OWASP/ASVS/master/5.0/docs_en/OWASP_Application_Security_Verification_Standard_5.0.0_en.json)).

### Stack-layer sources

Every named stack element has permissively licensed official docs, all prose without stable rule IDs, so each needs manual decomposition and a commit-hash pin **[P for licenses]**: go.dev content is CC BY 4.0 ([go.dev](https://go.dev/copyright)); Rust API Guidelines are Apache-2.0 or MIT ([repo](https://github.com/rust-lang/api-guidelines)); ANSSI's Secure Rust Guidelines use the French Open Licence 2.0 ([repo](https://github.com/ANSSI-FR/rust-guide)); react.dev is CC-BY-4.0 ([repo](https://github.com/reactjs/react.dev)); TanStack is MIT ([repo](https://github.com/TanStack/router)); Supabase is Apache-2.0 at repo root, with no separate docs license seen ([repo](https://github.com/supabase/supabase)). The two Rust guides are the only stack sources already near rule-per-record form **[I]**. Not covered: TypeScript handbook, Node.js, MDN, Vite, hosting hardening docs, PostgreSQL docs, Python beyond packaging.

### Cite-only

CIS Benchmarks are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and CIS Controls CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, and CIS's non-member terms separately bar derivative works, web posting and transfer of rights **[P]** ([CIS terms](https://www.cisecurity.org/terms-of-use-for-non-member-cis-products)); commercial use requires Product Vendor membership at contact-sales pricing **[P]** ([CIS](https://www.cisecurity.org/cis-securesuite/pricing-and-categories/product-vendor/)). PCI SSC's site terms restrict content to personal, non-commercial study and forbid derivative works **[P]** ([PCI SSC](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/terms_and_conditions/)); the PCI DSS version (v4.0.1 per search results) is **[U]**. The SOC 2 criteria sit behind an AICPA login in a file labelled as carrying copyright information **[P]**, with the wording itself **[U]** ([AICPA](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/download/2017-trust-services-criteria-with-revised-points-of-focus-2022)). ISO copyright wording for 27001 and 42001 is **[U]** (ISO pages errored). The Secure Controls Framework is CC BY-ND 4.0 with an explicit bar on AI-generated derivatives, and commercial licenses start at $25,000 a year, limited to GRC platforms **[P]** ([SCF](https://securecontrolsframework.com/commercial-license)). For all of these the safe record is identifier, short title, URL and an independently written rule mapped to it **[I]**.

### No DISA baseline exists for the target languages or for AI

A pass over the mirror's 1,101 titles found no STIG or SRG naming Go, Rust, Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Node, React, AI, machine learning or LLM; the only language-runtime STIGs are legacy .NET Framework 4.0 and Oracle JRE **[S]** ([cyber.trackr.live](https://cyber.trackr.live/stig/index)). This negative finding rests on a third-party mirror plus two web searches and needs confirmation against the October 2026 compilation. FY2026 NDAA Section 1512 on DoD AI/ML security surfaced but was not read, so a future DISA AI SRG is plausible and unevidenced **[I]** ([AGORA](https://agora.eto.tech/instrument/2691)).

One correction to the project's earlier notes: the STIG Manager documentation's "republished" refers to DISA re-issuing a rule under the same Rule ID with different content, and it points to the STIG support desk for the policy without stating it **[P]** ([STIG Manager](https://stig-manager.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user-guide/rule-exceptions.html)). It is not evidence of a DISA position on third-party republication; it is evidence that Rule IDs are not immutable content keys.

## A lean schema mapped to XCCDF holds, with seven caveats

XCCDF 1.2 (NIST IR 7275 Rev. 4, March 2012 errata edition) remains current; nothing newer was found **[P]** ([CSRC](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/7275/r4/upd1/final)). The researcher read roughly the first 200,000 of 233,935 characters through a summarising fetch tool, so table and section numbers need a spot-check against the PDF and XSD before publication.

| stigops-AI field | XCCDF 1.2 target | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | `Rule/@id` inside `xccdf_<ns>_rule_<name>` | Opaque IDs fit; XCCDF constrains only the wrapper |
| `title` | `title` | None |
| `severity` | `Rule/@severity` | Five values (unknown, info, low, medium, high); no "critical" |
| `source` | `reference` (Dublin Core + `@href`); `ident/@system` for scheme IDs | Store ASVS references with version |
| `extends` | `Rule/@extends` | Single parent; resolved at load time; Group extension deprecated |
| "Simple" | `description`, `rationale` | DISA overloads `description` with embedded sub-fields |
| "How to tell" | `check` (`@system`, content or ref) | `check` and `complex-check` are mutually exclusive |
| "What to say" | `fixtext`; executable fix in `fix/@system` | `strategy`, `disruption`, `complexity`, `reboot` enums reusable |
| Profile tailoring | `Profile` selectors; `Tailoring` document | See shadowing rule below |
| `layer`, `trigger`, `feedback`, Rot guards | None | Carry in `metadata`; export is lossy |

The seven caveats, all from the spec text as read **[P unless marked]** ([IR 7275r4 PDF](https://csrc.nist.gov/files/pubs/ir/7275/r4/upd1/final/docs/nistir-7275r4_updated-march-2012_clean.pdf)):

1. **Copy the merge model, not just the names.** Table 33 defines five per-property inheritance behaviours: None (id, status, extends), Prepend, Append (ident, fix, requires, conflicts, selectors), Replace (severity, weight, check, role, selected) and Override (title, description, rationale, warning, reference, fixtext, which append unless the child sets `override="true"`). A home-grown "more specific wins" that ignores this diverges on exactly the prose fields.
2. **Tailoring shadowing is strict.** A tailoring Profile replaces a benchmark Profile only when both its `id` and its `extends` equal that Profile's id; reusing the id without extending is an error, and tailoring Profiles cannot themselves be extended.
3. **Four fields have no home.** `layer` approximates Group nesting, `trigger` is not `platform` (which is CPE applicability, not an event), and `feedback` and Rot guards map only loosely to `status/@date`, `version/@time` and `warning`.
4. **"Must record why" is unconfirmed.** Whether `select`, `refine-rule` and `refine-value` accept a child `remark` is **[U]**; check Table 22 and the XSD. MITRE Vulcan's status-plus-justification model is a precedent **[P]** ([Vulcan](https://vulcan.mitre.org/disa-process/field-requirements)).
5. **DISA's usage is a narrow, flat subset.** One Group per Rule, lineage in titles and "Satisfies: SRG-…" prose, and pseudo-XML inside `description` (VulnDiscussion and others) that needs a second-stage parser **[P via tooling docs]** ([stig-parser](https://pypi.org/project/stig-parser), [SAF training](https://mitre.github.io/saf-training-current/courses/beginner/11.html)). That DISA never uses `extends`, and the exact `ident` system URI and description tag list, are **[U]**; no DISA XML was opened. Base-rule inheritance must be reconstructed from the SRG ID.
6. **Never key on the SV- rule ID.** DISA carries V- (vulnerability), SV-…r…_rule (revision-stamped) and the STIG ID in `version`; store all three as aliases and key imports on rule ID plus benchmark version/release or a content hash **[I, supported by the STIG Manager finding]**.
7. **Severity mapping is CAT I/II/III to high/medium/low** **[P]** ([Vulcan](https://vulcan.mitre.org/disa-process/field-requirements)).

Skipping OSCAL wholesale is defensible. OSCAL v1.2.3 (7 August 2026) is a control-catalog and authorization-package exchange format with mandatory UUIDs and metadata blocks, and its catalog has no native check or fix element **[P for version and metadata; the no-check claim is researcher analysis]** ([OSCAL releases](https://www.github.com/usnistgov/OSCAL/releases), [profile reference](https://pages.nist.gov/OSCAL-Reference/models/v1.2.3/profile/xml-reference/)). FedRAMP's RFC-0024 proposal accepts OSCAL as one format among others, lowering the risk of deferring it; whether it was finalised is **[U]** ([RFC-0024](https://www.fedramp.gov/rfcs/0024)). Stable IDs, scheme-qualified sources and generated JSON keep an OSCAL export cheap later **[I]**.

### Tooling precedents and one disagreement with the plan

ComplianceAsCode uses a directory-per-rule layout with `rule.yml` and co-located `oval/`, `bash/`, `ansible/` and `tests/`, plus `shared.<ext>` versus `<product>.<ext>` naming for scoped variants **[P]** ([CaC guide](https://complianceascode.readthedocs.io/en/latest/manual/developer/06_contributing_with_content.html)). Scorecard probes and Agent Skills use the same one-unit-one-folder shape **[P]** ([Agent Skills spec](https://agentskills.io/specification.md)). The documented pain is what CaC stacked on top, Jinja templating, XSLT and per-product builds, with MITRE's cac-builder pitch citing 30 to 60 minute builds; those numbers come from a tool selling the fix **[P, self-interested]** ([cac-builder](https://mitre.github.io/cac-builder/presentations/cac-builder-intro.html)). Lessons: keep rule files literal, state one precedence rule, put shared helpers outside rule folders, derive indexes by scanning **[I]**. InSpec has the cleanest overlay (a wrapper profile re-opens a named control) **[P]** ([InSpec](https://docs.chef.io/inspec/profiles/depends)); Semgrep has no inheritance; Kyverno 1.19 deprecating its legacy policy types shows the cost of tying rule format to an engine **[P]** ([Kyverno](https://kyverno.io/blog/2026/08/20/announcing-kyverno-release-1.19)). Agent Skills requires `name` to equal the directory name in lowercase alphanumerics and single hyphens, max 64 characters, which opaque IDs satisfy if they fit that character set.

No XCCDF-to-Markdown-with-frontmatter converter exists. SAF CLI converts XCCDF to InSpec stubs and HDF, and its `saf generate delta` (match by ID, then SRG/CCI, emit a report) is the design to copy for keeping opaque IDs stable across STIG releases **[P]** ([SAF CLI](https://saf-cli.mitre.org/)). stig-parser last released in May 2022 **[P]**.

**Disagreement with the plan.** The project specifies one repo per baseline linked as optional submodules. The formats researcher's inference favours a single repository: CaC and ASVS are single repos, and rules that inherit from a shared base turn every `extends` into a cross-repo pin when split **[I]** ([CaC](https://github.com/ComplianceAsCode/content)). The counter-case is real: InSpec's dependency model is built for multi-repo with pinned git refs **[P]**, per-source repos enforce the share-alike separation that licensing demands, and MITRE's one-repo-per-baseline pattern is **[U]**. This is a design tension to decide, not a settled finding.

## Raw STIG lookup is taken; the four-layer join is not

Three services already hand STIG data to software, two of them to AI agents for free. STIGViewer/OpenControls.ai (MoxyWolf LLC) sells normalized STIG JSON by REST API at **$1,500 a year per organization**, with no MCP and no free tier mentioned **[P, vendor-stated coverage]** ([press release](https://www.stigviewer.com/press-releases/2026-03-16)). MacTech Solutions runs a hosted, no-login STIG MCP with 2,029 rules across 15 benchmarks, registered 9 August 2026 **[P, vendor-stated]** ([MacTech](https://www.mactechsolutionsllc.com/mcp/stig)). `jeneric/stig-mcp` is a local MIT server chaining ATT&CK to 800-53 to STIG steps, first released 30 September 2026 **[P]** ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/stig-mcp/)). Two STIGViewer conflicts are unreconciled: "about 500 profiles, quarterly" in the press release versus "250+, weekly sync" on the explainer, and MoxyWolf ownership versus a Unified Compliance page claiming it ([explainer](https://stigviewer.com/explainer), [UC](https://unifiedcompliance.com/stigviewer)). STIGViewer's roadmap toward "atomic requirement decomposition" moves it toward a normalized library, for GRC vendors **[S, vendor roadmap]**.

Adjacent occupants, each partial **[P unless noted]**:

| Project | What it has | What it lacks |
|---|---|---|
| Project CodeGuard (CoSAI/OASIS) | CC BY 4.0 security rules for AI coding agents, self-hosted MCP | Security only; no stack profiles; developer audience ([repo](https://github.com/cosai-oasis/project-codeguard)) |
| AppGenie compliance-mcp | Hosted, metered, cited; an "AI-Controlled Delivery" profile | Framework profiles not tech stacks; GRC audience ([site](https://appgenie.com.au/compliance-mcp)) |
| GuardVibe | Local MCP scanner, 300+ rules incl. Supabase, compliance mapping (vendor-stated) | No cited corpus; scanner, not guidance ([mcp.so](https://mcp.so/server/guardvibe/goklab)) |
| Context7 | The delivery model; 64.4k stars | Library docs only ([repo](https://github.com/upstash/context7)) |
| Ansvar security-controls-mcp | SCF crosswalk over MCP | Data is CC BY-ND; no AI derivatives ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/security-controls-mcp/1.0.0/)) |
| Lovable, Replit built-ins | Non-developer audience, scans | Platform-locked, after the fact ([Lovable](https://lovable.dev/blog/secure-vibe-coding), [Replit](https://docs.replit.com/features/security/agent-security-scans.md)) |

Official MCP Registry searches for "secure coding" and "best practices" returned zero results; other compliance entries (Bidda's claimed 10,065 nodes, MEOK's NIST and OWASP servers) were listed but not inspected and could be closer than scored **[P for the search; [U] for those servers]** ([registry](https://registry.modelcontextprotocol.io/v0/servers?search=compliance&limit=40&version=latest)). The spec-driven frameworks (superpowers ~316k stars, Spec Kit ~146k, OpenSpec ~73k, BMAD ~55k) sell process to developers and none carries a versioned, cited standards corpus at README level **[P, README-level only]** ([superpowers](https://github.com/obra/superpowers), [spec-kit](https://github.com/github/spec-kit)).

**The gap that remains [I]:** four layers in place of security alone, profiles keyed to a technology stack instead of a compliance framework, a non-developer as end reader, and STIG/SRG association as an applicability notice for self-hosted systems. Each element exists somewhere; the join does not. The gap is narrow in engineering terms: CodeGuard adding stack packs, GuardVibe adding citations, or Context7 adding a standards content type would each close much of it. The absence claim rests on registry keyword searches and a handful of web searches; Glama, PulseMCP and mcp.so were not exhaustively crawled.

Two distribution and pricing facts bear on the plan. Vercel reports that skills.sh reached one million skills and nearly 280 million installs in about seven months, with the top 375 skills taking 62% of installs **[P, vendor-stated]** ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/state-of-agent-skills)); the researcher infers that skills, not MCP alone, are where this audience installs things. Context7 charges $10 per seat per month after a free 1,000 calls a month with 20 bonus calls a day once exhausted, and requires a free account **[P]** ([pricing](https://context7.com/docs/plans-pricing.md)); an archived older version showed a 60-requests-per-hour model, date unknown. No precedent was found for a daily tool-call paywall aimed at non-developers. Because the corpus is public, the paid thing can only be hosted retrieval and normalization, which caps pricing power **[I]**.

## No upstream pushes; a polling registry with hashes carries the load

GitHub Atom feeds and the GitHub API were blocked from the research sandbox, so every `releases.atom` and `commits.atom` pattern below is **[U]**; raw files and HTML pages were reachable.

| Upstream | How it publishes | Best signal |
|---|---|---|
| DISA | Quarterly compilation ZIP plus out-of-cycle single releases; site is a client-rendered Salesforce shell; old RSS path ends at a DoD login | HEAD on `U_SRG-STIG_Library_<Month>_<Year>.zip`; mirror API cross-check |
| NIST | oscal-content repo; drafts-open-for-comment JSON/XML; nist.gov RSS; GovDelivery email; undocumented CPRT export | Commit SHA + file hash; CPRT hash with size assertion |
| OWASP | ASVS tags; Top 10 repo; GenAI WordPress RSS; Cheat Sheets rolling | Tag where one exists; per-file SHA + text hash otherwise |
| AI protocol specs | MCP dated tags, per-version changelog, SEP index, six-month cadence; A2A CHANGELOG; llms.txt on most | Releases + changelog hash + llms.txt hash |
| Platform docs | llms.txt on all eight checked; changelog RSS on Lovable, Bolt, Cursor, Claude Code, Supabase; none found for Replit, v0 | RSS newest-item compare; `.md` page hash |
| MCP registries | Official registry: free, unauthenticated, `updated_since`; Glama and PulseMCP need keys | Hourly incremental poll of the official registry |

The DISA findings matter most **[P, probed]**. The October 2026 library ZIP returned 200 at 375,827,141 bytes with Last-Modified 26 October 2026 ([ZIP](https://dl.dod.cyber.mil/wp-content/uploads/stigs/zip/U_SRG-STIG_Library_October_2026.zip)). The January and April 2026 ZIPs and an April 2025 PDF all show Last-Modified 28 April 2026, which the researcher reads as a bulk re-upload during the site migration **[I]**; the consequence is that Last-Modified is not a trustworthy release date, and the pin should be Content-Length plus SHA-256 with the release date taken from the XCCDF inside. The server honoured If-Modified-Since but not If-None-Match. Every documented downstream tool (CaC, STIG Manager, SAF) ingests DISA content from a file a human downloaded **[P]** ([STIG Manager admin guide](https://stig-manager.readthedocs.io/en/latest/admin-guide/admin-guide.html)), and Ansible-Lockdown states a lag of about 30 days to subscribers and months to the public **[P]** ([release schedule](https://mpg-al-readthedocs.readthedocs.io/en/main/combined/release_sched.html)). Automatic DISA release detection would itself differentiate **[I]**. cyber.trackr.live offers a free JSON API listing every STIG with version and date, with ETags, though how it ingests is unpublished ([API](https://cyber.trackr.live/api)).

Other verified details: the old CSRC metafeed RSS URLs return 404; the CPRT export returns HTTP 200 with a 44-byte empty body for a wrong ID and carries no validators ([drafts feed](https://csrc.nist.gov/CSRC/media/feeds/pubs/drafts-open-for-comment.json)). MCP's latest stable is `2026-07-28`, and SEP-3398 (accepted 14 October 2026) sets a six-month cadence, moving the next target to late March 2027 ([MCP releases](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/releases), [SEP-3398](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/seps/3398-release-cycle-updates.md)). The official registry's own guide tells aggregators to poll about hourly and warns of possible data resets while in preview ([guide](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/registry/registry-aggregators.md)). Glama's data requires visible attribution on every view ([Glama ToS](https://glama.ai/policies/terms-of-service)); PulseMCP's v0beta API returns 410 since September 2026.

### The monitoring design the notes converge on [I]

A single `sources.yaml` registry holds one row per upstream artifact: detector type (HTTP HEAD, conditional GET, RSS, sitemap lastmod, GitHub release, GitHub path commits, registry `updated_since`, JSON hash, headless), URL, cadence and a sanity rule (minimum bytes or a required marker). One scheduled runner executes detectors and commits `state/<source>.json` back to git, so history is the audit trail and a state change triggers the rebuild. Cadence is tiered: hourly for the MCP registry; daily for releases, the DISA ZIP HEAD, changelog RSS and llms.txt hashes; weekly for sitemap sweeps, CPRT hashes and a headless render of cyber.mil. The change decision has two stages: deterministic first (validator or normalized-text SHA-256 differs and the sanity rule passes), then an LLM labels the proven diff as substantive, editorial or noise and lists affected rule IDs. The model never decides whether a change exists. The system alerts on silence: empty-but-200 bodies, login redirects, archived or moved repos (the OWASP LLM Top 10 move is a live example), and any detector that has not succeeded within twice its cadence.

Context7's published refresh policy supplies three rules worth copying **[P]** ([library updates](https://context7.com/docs/library-updates.md)): never replace a good snapshot with a degraded fetch (it requires a re-crawl to reach 10% of the prior page count), keep the previous major version addressable, and show a notice when a source is unreachable. Its "ContextCrush" incident, where user-supplied rules were passed unchanged to coding agents and could instruct them to exfiltrate `.env` files, was reported 18 February 2026 and fixed 23 February **[P]** ([write-up](https://context7.com/guides/contextcrush)). stigops-AI output reaches coding agents the same way, so upstream free text needs injection screening before it ships.

For per-rule provenance, SLSA and in-toto converge on an immutable input identifier (URI plus digest), the producer and the time **[P]** ([in-toto statement](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/in-toto/attestation/main/spec/v1/statement.md), [SLSA v1.1](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.1/provenance)). The suggested block: source URI, download location, upstream version label, published date, raw SHA-256, normalized-text SHA-256, locator, git commit, retrieved-at, derived-by (pipeline and model ID), verified-by and verified-at, status and supersedes **[I]**. GitHub's 60-requests-per-hour unauthenticated and 5,000 authenticated limits are **[P]** ([GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/using-the-rest-api/rate-limits-for-the-rest-api)); Actions cron caveats (delays, auto-disable after inactivity) are **[U]** and should be checked before relying on Actions alone.

## Findings that change the plan

- **STIG lookup is not the product.** A free hosted STIG MCP and a free local one exist; the paid tier has to rest on stack applicability, the four-layer join and explanation.
- **Share-alike dictates packaging.** A per-record `license` field and per-source separation are required before any OWASP import; this also bears on the repo-per-baseline decision.
- **The DISA legal basis is open.** Public-domain status and modification rights are unconfirmed from DISA; the earlier STIG Manager citation does not support a republication policy. Email disa.stig_spt@mail.mil or read the FAQ in a browser.
- **Key imports on more than Rule ID.** DISA re-issues rules under the same ID; store V-, SV- and STIG ID as aliases with version/release or a content hash.
- **Copy XCCDF Table 33 and the shadowing rule,** and accept lossy export for `layer`, `trigger`, `feedback` and Rot guards.
- **Monorepo versus submodules is a live tension** between inheritance (favours one repo) and license separation (favours many).
- **Build the DISA detector.** ZIP HEAD plus hash plus mirror cross-check; no downstream tool automates this.
- **Screen upstream text for injected instructions** before serving it to coding agents.
- **Add a skills listing alongside MCP,** and note that a daily-limit paywall has no precedent for this audience.
- **OWASP LLM Top 10 moved repos and reportedly has a 2026 edition;** SSDF and AI RMF are both mid-revision. Pin and expect re-maps.
- **Regulatory content must quote official text with a date,** since trackers disagree on dates and counts, and 2 December 2026 is the next reported EU milestone.
